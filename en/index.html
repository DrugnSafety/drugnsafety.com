Our Mission

We move the record of a drug reaction out of one hospital's chart and into the patient's hands, and turn it into guidance any clinic or pharmacy can act on.

Why it matters

The record stays at the hospital.
The patient is left with memory.

A patient who has had an adverse drug reaction is prescribed the same drug again at another hospital. The body that reacted belongs to the patient, but the record belongs to the hospital.

  • It breaks when the hospital changes

    The history lives on a paper card and in memory, so it does not reach the next hospital or pharmacy.

  • Prescription checks do not have it

    Korea's Drug Utilization Review (DUR), the prescription check run by the Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service, holds no individual allergy history.

  • Every patient is different

    Even with the same NSAID allergy, a drug that is fine for one patient can be dangerous for another.

A National Health Insurance Service analysis estimated 430,000 patients with adverse drug reactions in Korea in 2014, at a socioeconomic cost of KRW 535.2 billion.

01 · Record

A card you always carry, a record that opens by QR

The front of the card shows the culprit drug and the reaction, with contraindicated, caution and safe-to-take drugs marked like a traffic light. Scanning the QR code opens the full record, down to causality and severity.

  • Mapped to HL7 FHIR AllergyIntolerance so that hospitals can exchange it.
  • For patients who find apps hard, the card image and its link can also be sent as a KakaoTalk alert message.

From: Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS) R&D, 2022 - 2024, led by Chungbuk National University Hospital. The My ADR platform at myadr.care

02 · Check

Every prescribed drug, checked in one scan

Scan the QR code at the bottom of a prescription and each drug is checked against your record for anything risky. You can also search one drug at a time by brand or ingredient name.

  • For a contraindicated drug, the app also tells you to talk to the prescribing doctor.

03 · Guide

Rules decide. AI writes. Clinicians approve.

When a patient uploads their own health record, a rule engine computes the polypharmacy risks and a multi-agent LLM turns the result into a guide the patient can read. Only a guide that a clinician has reviewed and approved reaches the patient.

  • Screens for drug interactions, potentially inappropriate medications for older adults, anticholinergic burden, and 146 rules based on STOPP/START version 3.
  • Built on the principle of writing nothing without evidence. The guide generator in development in 2026 uses only the contraindicated, caution and safe-to-take information stated in the record.
  • Three reports: a polypharmacy reconciliation guide, a Smart Allergy Report, and tailored medication counseling.

From: Ministry of Health and Welfare (MOHW) healthcare MyData R&D, 2023 - 2025, led by Chungbuk National University Hospital. Field validation at 3 institutions

FAQ

What people ask

Can I use it now?

The Drug Allergy Alert app came out of research at Chungbuk National University Hospital in 2018 and is on Google Play. It is for patients whose care team has registered their allergy information. The My ADR platform (myadr.care) has been used by the hospitals that took part in the research. The combined Drug&Safety service is in preparation, and institutions that want to adopt it can write to us.

Who enters my allergy information?

Your care team registers the contraindicated, caution and safe-to-take drugs they confirmed in clinic. Every verdict the app and the card show rests on that record.

Does the AI decide whether a drug is safe?

No. Verdicts come from the registered record and the rule engine. The LLM turns that result into text a patient can read, and a guide reaches the patient only after a clinician has reviewed and approved it. It is designed not to guess beyond the record.

Can another hospital or pharmacy see it?

Scanning the QR code on the card opens the full record your care team registered. When needed, the record can be passed to clinicians in the HL7 FHIR international standard format.

How does a hospital adopt it?

Write to us. The research piloted a link in which the hospital EMR fills in the information-sharing form and sends it to the platform over an API.

Are the patients and records on this page real?

The patients and records in the demo, the card and the films are all fictional examples. The image marked as a real screen shows nothing that identifies a patient. This page does not provide medical advice. Please talk to your care team before making decisions about medication.
