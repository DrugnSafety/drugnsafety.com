Every new prescription still relies on your memory.

Your body had the reaction, but the record stays with the hospital that saw it. Change hospital or pharmacy and you start explaining from scratch.

  • It breaks when you change hospitals

    A history kept on a paper card or in your head does not follow you to the next hospital or pharmacy.

  • Prescription checks cannot see it

    Korea's national prescription check (DUR) holds no personal drug allergy history.

  • The answer differs from person to person

    With the same painkiller allergy, a drug that is fine for one person can be dangerous for another, so people end up avoiding medicines they need.

A National Health Insurance Service analysis estimated 430,000 patients with adverse drug reactions in 2014 alone, at a socioeconomic cost of 535.2 billion won.

From record to answer in three steps

  1. 1

    Bring your record together

    We use the reactions confirmed in clinic together with the diagnoses, prescriptions and test results you download from the government’s My Health Record app.

  2. 2

    Rules screen, clinicians confirm

    Explicit rules screen first for contraindications, interactions and drugs that need care in older adults. AI drafts the plain-language wording, and the process is designed for a clinician to review and approve it.

  3. 3

    Get your card and guide

    Receive your QR drug safety card and medication guide in an app, by KakaoTalk message or as a PDF. At a pharmacy or hospital, just show the QR.

Four things you keep

Each was built and used with patients in research the founder led. We are now bringing them together as one service.

Digital drug safety card · My ADR

One QR code lets you show your record at a new hospital.

  • The front shows the culprit drug, the reaction, and what is contraindicated, needs caution or is OK to take, in traffic-light colors.
  • The QR code opens the full record: onset date, severity, causality and confirmatory tests.
  • Depending on the hospital, you receive it as a plastic card, in an app or by KakaoTalk message.
  • It is mapped to the HL7 FHIR international standard so that hospitals can exchange the record.

Drug Allergy Alert · Android app

Before you pick up a medicine, check it against your record.

  • Search a drug or ingredient name and see contraindicated, caution or OK to take, based on your record.
  • Scan the 2D code printed at the bottom of a prescription to check every prescribed drug against your record at once. If a code is not recognized, the app asks you to search by name.
  • If a drug is contraindicated, the app tells you to talk to the prescribing doctor.
  • An action plan spells out what to do, such as “avoid NSAIDs, including patches and eye drops”.

MyData medication guide · SMART DUR CDSS

Medicines from several hospitals, sorted onto one page.

  • It starts from your diagnoses, the last 12 months of prescriptions, and test and checkup results downloaded from My Health Record.
  • Clinicians also review how regularly each medicine was refilled over the past year (PDC).
  • For each medicine it explains in plain words why you take it, what to watch for, and which symptoms mean you should talk to your doctor.
  • Guides are designed to reach the patient only after a clinician has reviewed and approved them.

What the guide covers

  1. Each current medicine
  2. Drugs that need special care
  3. Drugs to take steadily
  4. Combinations to watch
  5. Symptoms to look out for
  6. Summary and advice

Example: warfarin with ibuprofen goes under “combinations to watch”, because the pair can raise the risk of bleeding.

Personal drug allergy guide · SDAP

Leave with the words to use at the pharmacy and the ER.

  • Drug classes are sorted into contraindicated, caution and OK to take, each with the reason.
  • Explanations are written in plain words for your age, sex and other conditions.
  • Each class comes with at least three answers to questions such as “What do I tell the pharmacist?” and “What can I take instead?”
  • It is designed to go out as a PDF with a QR card only after an allergist has reviewed and approved it.
Pilot
Guides were produced for all 40 patients in an allergy clinic pilot at Chungbuk National University Hospital. Single-center pilot, patient-reported on a 5-point scale: understanding of their own allergy 4.6, confidence managing their medicines 4.4, confidence telling clinicians about their allergy 4.5 (poster abstract submitted to a 2026 congress).

AI never decides alone whether you can take a drug.

The starting point is your clinician-verified record and explicit rules. AI drafts the plain-language wording, and the text that reaches you is designed to be reviewed and approved by a clinician.

  1. Your recordReactions, diagnoses, prescriptions
  2. Rule engineInteractions, older-adult cautions, STOPP/START
  3. AI draftFindings in plain words
  4. Clinician approvalReviewed, edited, approved
  5. To youApp, KakaoTalk, PDF

  • No guessing beyond your record

    The system is designed to work from the registered contraindicated, caution and OK-to-take lists. If a drug is not on record, it says so and asks you to check with your care team.

  • Designed for clinician approval

    AI drafts are designed to be reviewed and edited by a clinician before they reach you.

  • Your record stays yours

    You can show the QR card at any hospital or pharmacy and, when needed, pass the record to clinicians in HL7 FHIR format. The sharing standard was adopted for a Ministry of Health and Welfare pilot program.

Drugs are coded with ATC and KD codes, reactions with MedDRA and severity with CTCAE.

Questions

Can I use it now?

The apps and platform from the research have been used by patients at the participating hospitals. Drug Allergy Alert is on Google Play, and the digital drug safety card was issued at hospitals in the research. The combined Drug&Safety service, open to anyone, is being prepared. Join the waitlist and you will hear first.

What do I get by joining the waitlist?

We will email you when the service opens and how to use it. Joining is free.

Who enters my allergy information?

Clinicians register the contraindicated, caution and OK-to-take drugs confirmed in clinic. Your current medicines and diagnoses come from the record you download yourself from My Health Record.

Does AI decide whether I can take a drug?

No. AI does not decide whether you can take a drug. The starting point is the record your clinician registered and explicit rules, and AI drafts the plain-language text. In the allergy guide AI also drafts the grouping of drug classes, so the guide is designed to go out only after a clinician has reviewed and approved it. The system is designed not to guess anything that is not on record.

Can other hospitals and pharmacies see it?

Scanning the QR code on your card opens the detailed record your clinician registered. When needed, the record can be passed to clinicians in the HL7 FHIR international format.

Does it work abroad?

In a research demo we tested showing the card in English and Chinese to clinicians abroad. We will tell waitlist members when it becomes part of the service.

How is my personal information handled?

The waitlist asks for your email address, plus the role, interests and optional name you choose to fill in, and uses them only for waitlist updates. Please do not send medical records or prescription photos.

How can a hospital or pharmacy adopt it?

Choose “clinician or institution” on the waitlist form or email us. In the research, hospitals piloted sending the standard sharing form from their medical records to the platform through an API.

Are the patients and records on this page real?

All patients and records in the demo and the card are fictional examples. Images marked as real screens show 2018 demo records and contain no identifying information. This page does not provide medical advice. Talk to your care team before making decisions about medication.
